Trip Planning Guide

The Best Time to
Visit India

India doesn't have one season — it has a dozen, depending on where you stand. Here's what to expect, month by month and region by region, so your dates match your destination.

Short answer: October to March is kind to most of the country — cool, dry, and clear. But the Himalayas wake up in summer, Kerala turns lush in monsoon, and the desert is best avoided in May. Scroll on for the season your trip actually needs.

Oct – Mar
Peak Season

Winter

The most comfortable stretch of the year across the plains and the south. Days are warm, nights are cool, and skies stay clear — ideal for forts, cities, wildlife parks, and the backwaters alike. This is when most travellers come, so book heritage hotels early.

  • Temp: 10°C – 25°C in the north, 20°C – 32°C in the south
  • Best for: Rajasthan, Delhi–Agra, Kerala, wildlife safaris, Goa's dry beaches
  • Watch out: Dense fog can delay early flights in Delhi and Punjab, December–January
Apr – Jun
Mountain Season

Summer

The plains turn hot and best avoided, but this is exactly when the high Himalayas open up. Snow clears from mountain passes, Ladakh becomes reachable by road, and hill stations offer a genuine escape from the heat below.

  • Temp: 35°C – 45°C in the plains, 10°C – 22°C in the hills
  • Best for: Ladakh, Spiti, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Kashmir
  • Watch out: Central and North Indian cities get uncomfortably hot by May
Jul – Sep
Green Season

Monsoon

Often skipped, often underrated. The rains turn the Western Ghats, Kerala, and the Northeast a vivid green, waterfalls run full, and crowds thin out everywhere. It's the off-season price advantage with genuinely dramatic scenery — if you don't mind rescheduling around rain.

  • Temp: 25°C – 33°C, high humidity
  • Best for: Kerala's Ayurveda season, Western Ghats, Meghalaya, Goa's off-season calm
  • Watch out: Landslide risk on mountain roads; carry buffer days into any itinerary
